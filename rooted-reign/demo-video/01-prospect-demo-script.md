# Prospect Demo Video Script (3 to 5 minutes)

**Who gets this:** only owners who replied to the cold email. Never attach it to a cold email.
**Target length:** about 4 minutes (roughly 600 spoken words at a relaxed pace).
**Format:** screen recording with Trenna's face in a corner bubble (Loom or similar).
**Status:** DRAFT. Trenna approves before any video is recorded or sent.

---

## Before you record: personalization checklist (5 minutes per prospect)

Fill these in from what you actually see. If you did not check something, leave it out of the script. Do not guess.

| Field | Where to find it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `[FIRST NAME]` | Their reply | Marco |
| `[BUSINESS]` | Their reply or website | Valley Comfort Air |
| `[TRADE]` | Website | HVAC |
| `[CITY]` | Google Business Profile | Mesa |
| `[WHAT I SAW]` | Their website and Google listing | "Your site has a contact form but no chat, and the form doesn't say when you'll reply." |
| `[AFTER-HOURS TEST]` | Optional: call their main line after 6pm | "I called at 6:40 last night and got voicemail." |
| `[SPANISH SIGNAL]` | Reviews, site, service area | "I saw 2 of your reviews are in Spanish." |
| `[THEIR WORDS]` | Their reply email | "You said you miss calls when you're on a roof." |

**Demo setup tip:** set the demo line's greeting to their business name before you record, so the call they watch sounds like their own phone being answered. Reset it after.

**Have open on screen, in this order:**
1. Their website (tab 1)
2. Demo phone or softphone showing the incoming call and live transcript (tab 2)
3. The text message thread on the demo phone (tab 3)
4. The CRM contact card and pipeline (tab 4)
5. The calendar showing the booked slot (tab 5)

## The script

### 0:00 to 0:25 | Open on their website

> Hi `[FIRST NAME]`, it's Trenna. Thanks for writing back.
>
> I'm on `[BUSINESS]`'s website right now. `[WHAT I SAW]`
>
> `[THEIR WORDS]` So I built this short video around exactly that. About 4 minutes.

### 0:25 to 0:55 | The 6pm problem

> Here's the situation I want to show you.
>
> It's 6pm. Your crew is finishing a job across town. A homeowner in `[CITY]` has a problem and they're calling the first `[TRADE]` company they find.
>
> If nobody picks up, that homeowner usually isn't going to sit and wait for a call back. They call the next company. That's not a marketing problem. That's a job you already paid to get, walking out the door.
>
> *(Optional, only if you did the test:)* `[AFTER-HOURS TEST]`
>
> So let's call your line at 6pm and see what happens with the system in place.

### 0:55 to 1:50 | Live call in English

**Switch to tab 2. Place the call on camera.**

> I'm calling now. Watch the transcript on the right.

Let the call play. Sample flow for the demo line (adjust the trade):

> **System:** Thanks for calling `[BUSINESS]`. This is the after-hours line. How can I help you tonight?
> **Caller:** Hi, my AC stopped blowing cold air and it's still 98 degrees in the house.
> **System:** I'm sorry, that's miserable in this heat. Let's get you on the schedule. Can I get your name and the address where you need service?
> **Caller:** Dana Ruiz, 1420 East Main Street.
> **System:** Thanks, Dana. Is the unit making any noise, or just blowing warm air?
> **Caller:** Just warm air.
> **System:** Got it. I have tomorrow at 8am or 10am open. Which works better?
> **Caller:** 8am.
> **System:** You're booked for tomorrow at 8am. You'll get a text confirmation in a moment, and a reminder before the tech arrives.

**Point at the screen while you talk:**

> Notice what it did. It answered on the first ring. It got the name, the address, and what's wrong. It booked a real slot on your calendar. And it sounded like a person who works for you, not a phone tree.

### 1:50 to 2:35 | Same call in Spanish

> Now here's the part most of your competitors can't do. Same call, but in Spanish.

**Place the second call. Show the transcript with the English translation next to it.**

> **Caller:** Hola, se me descompuso el aire acondicionado y la casa está muy caliente.
> **System:** Lamento mucho escucharlo. Con este calor es muy difícil. Vamos a programar una cita. ¿Me da su nombre y la dirección del servicio?
> **Caller:** Luis Herrera, 3310 West Baseline Road.
> **System:** Gracias, Luis. Tengo disponible mañana a las 8 o a las 10 de la mañana. ¿Cuál le queda mejor?
> **Caller:** A las 10.
> **System:** Listo. Queda programado para mañana a las 10. Le voy a mandar un mensaje de confirmación.

> It switched to Spanish on its own, because he spoke Spanish. No "press 2." And the job details land in your CRM in English, so you and your team can read them.
>
> *(If you have it:)* `[SPANISH SIGNAL]` So this isn't a nice extra for you. Those are customers you already have.

### 2:35 to 3:05 | Missed call text-back and web chat

**Switch to tab 3.**

> Sometimes a customer hangs up before anything answers. Watch this.
>
> I'll call and hang up after 1 ring.

**Hang up. Show the text arriving.**

> Within seconds they get a text: "Sorry we missed you. This is `[BUSINESS]`. What can we help with?" They reply by text and the system books them the same way.
>
> Same thing on your website. *(Switch to tab 1, open the chat bubble.)* Someone fills this out at 11pm, they get an answer at 11pm. Not the next morning.

### 3:05 to 3:35 | What you see as the owner

**Switch to tab 4, then tab 5.**

> Here's your side. Every call, text, and chat becomes a contact here with the job details and the recording. The booked ones are already on your calendar.
>
> The ones that didn't book don't get forgotten. The system follows up by text on its own until they book or tell you no. You don't have to remember who to call back. You just show up to the jobs.
>
> You don't log into anything new to make it work. I set it up and I manage it.

### 3:35 to 4:00 | The point

> Here's what I want you to take from this, `[FIRST NAME]`.
>
> The money is not in the tool. The money is in never losing a lead. You already pay for every call that comes in: your trucks, your reviews, your ads, your reputation. This just makes sure each one gets answered and followed up.

### 4:00 to 4:30 | Offer and next step

> Here's how it works.
>
> Setup is $997. I build it around your services, your schedule, and your service area, in English and Spanish.
>
> Then it's $397 a month, locked for life. That price is only for my first 3 clients. After that it goes to $497.
>
> If you want to be one of the 3, just reply to my email with "let's do it" and I'll send you a short setup form and a time to get started.
>
> Thanks for watching, `[FIRST NAME]`. Talk soon.

## Notes for Trenna

- **Keep the demo honest.** Only show features that are live in your demo account. If calendar booking isn't connected yet for a prospect's tool, say "it books into your calendar" only after you've confirmed it works with what they use.
- **The 98 degrees line** is a scripted scenario. Change it for winter or for other trades:
  - Plumbing: "water heater is leaking into the garage"
  - Roofing: "storm last night and there's a stain on the ceiling"
  - Landscaping: "my irrigation line broke and the yard is flooding"
  - Garage doors: "my door is stuck open and I can't leave for work"
- **Addresses and names** in the sample calls are made up for the demo. Don't present the calls as real customers.
- **The "they call the next company" line** is an inference from how people shop for urgent repairs, not a measured statistic. Don't attach a number to it unless you have a real source.
- **Cut for time:** if you run long, trim the web chat moment in the 2:35 section first. Never cut the Spanish call, the money point, or the offer.
