## The script

**Talking head.**

>
> If you don't answer, they don't wait. They go back to Google and call the next name on the list.
>

> You'll see the whole thing live: the call, the booking, the follow-up, and what you see as the owner.

### 1:15 to 3:00 | Why the first company to answer gets the job

**Talking head. Optional B-roll: phone ringing on a truck dashboard.**

> Let's talk about why this matters.
>
> When something breaks in someone's house, they aren't shopping for the best company. They're shopping for the first company that answers and says, "We can help."
>
> That's my read from how people buy urgent repairs. I'm not quoting a study. But think about the last time your own water heater went out. Did you call 1 company and wait patiently for a call back? Or did you keep calling until someone picked up?
>
> Here's where most small teams get stuck:
>
> - The owner is also the lead tech, so the phone goes to voicemail during jobs.
> - The website form sends an email that nobody sees until 9pm.
> - A lot of the Valley speaks Spanish, and if the person who answers doesn't, that customer may hang up.
> - And the leads that don't book right away? Nobody follows up, because everyone's busy.
>
> Hiring a front desk person costs real money, and they still go home at 5. So most owners just accept that they'll lose some calls.
>
> You don't have to accept that. Let me show you.

### 3:00 to 5:00 | Demo: the call in English

**Screen recording. Demo line, live transcript, calendar visible.**

> This is a demo line set up for a sample HVAC company. I'm going to call it right now, at 6pm, like a homeowner would.

**Play the call.**

> **System:** Thanks for calling Desert Valley Air. This is the after-hours line. How can I help you tonight?
> **Caller:** Hi, my AC stopped blowing cold air and it's still 98 degrees in the house.
> **System:** I'm sorry, that's miserable in this heat. Let's get you on the schedule. Can I get your name and the address where you need service?
> **Caller:** Dana Ruiz, 1420 East Main Street in Mesa.
> **System:** Thanks, Dana. Is the unit making any noise, or just blowing warm air?
> **Caller:** Just warm air.
> **System:** Got it. I have tomorrow at 8am or 10am open. Which works better?
> **Caller:** 8am.
> **System:** You're booked for tomorrow at 8am. You'll get a text confirmation in a moment, and a reminder before the tech arrives.

**Pause the recording. Point at the screen.**

> Let's break down what just happened.
>
> 1. It answered on the first ring.
> 2. It used the company name, so the caller knew they reached the right place.
> 3. It asked the questions a good dispatcher would ask: name, address, what's wrong.
> 4. It offered real open times from the calendar, and booked one.
> 5. It sent a text confirmation.
>
> Nobody on the crew touched their phone.

### 5:00 to 6:30 | Demo: the same call in Spanish

> Now the same situation, but the homeowner speaks Spanish.

**Play the call. Show English captions on screen.**

> **Caller:** Hola, se me descompuso el aire acondicionado y la casa está muy caliente.
> **System:** Lamento mucho escucharlo. Con este calor es muy difícil. Vamos a programar una cita. ¿Me da su nombre y la dirección del servicio?
> **Caller:** Luis Herrera, 3310 West Baseline Road.
> **System:** Gracias, Luis. Tengo disponible mañana a las 8 o a las 10 de la mañana. ¿Cuál le queda mejor?
> **Caller:** A las 10.
> **System:** Listo. Queda programado para mañana a las 10. Le voy a mandar un mensaje de confirmación.

> It heard Spanish and answered in Spanish. No menu, no "press 2."
>
> And here's the part owners like: the job notes land in the CRM in English. So if you don't speak Spanish, you can still read exactly what Luis needs before your tech shows up.
>
> If you serve Spanish-speaking neighborhoods, this is customers you already have, getting the same service as everyone else.

### 6:30 to 8:00 | Demo: missed call text-back and web chat

> Not every lead comes in as a full phone call. Some people hang up after 2 rings. Some never call at all. They fill out your website form at 11pm.

**Call and hang up. Show the text arrive.**

**Switch to the website. Open the chat.**

> Same thing on the website. Someone types, "Do you do tune-ups?" at 11pm. They get an answer at 11pm, and an offer to book. Not an email to you that sits until morning.
